Phase 4 details:

Here is my high level brainstorming.  Please review and evaluate, offering alternative strategies if appropriate.
* Some WorldObjects will have a list of behaviors that they can perform (we will call these Actors). For this phase, only NPC WorldObjects will have behaviors but eventually other 
WorldObjectKind types will have behaviors.  Examples of future types would be dungeon bosses, wandering unique mobs, NPC's who join the PC party (and would have their own "behavior" 
or scripts to drive combat and internal team conversations). 
These behaviors will be initially creating using the Templates class, eventually the WorldData object (which currently holds instances to all runtime game data) will 
be persisted/loaded as either YAML or JSON.
* Each behavior will have a trigger condition and an action. The trigger condition will be evaluated each turn and if it is true, the action will be performed.
* The trigger condition will be a simple expression that can reference the WorldObject's properties and the properties of other WorldObjects in the same location. 
For example, a trigger condition could be "if there is a player in the same location" or "if the NPC's health is below 50%".
* Actors that are in the same location (chunk) or in adjacent locations to the player will be processed each turn. This will allow for NPCs to react to the player's 
presence and actions in a timely manner, without having to process every single NPC in the game world every turn. 
* Actors outside of the player's location and adjacent locations will be processed less frequently, perhaps every 10 turns, to allow for behaviors that are not directly related to the 
player, such as wandering or patrolling.  Note that the Actor's behaviors in this situation are simply shortened, they will still end up completing the same actions, just at a faster rate
and with less impact on the CPU loop cost.  

Here are some examples of behaviors that NPCs could have:
NPC Name:  Weapons Blacksmith
Kind: NPC
Modes:  (what activity the NPC is currently performing, which can affect their behavior triggers and actions)
  - Idle
  - Forging (the blacksmith is forging weapons for customers, subtasks include heating, hammering, quenching, heating, hammering, quenching, heating, hammering, quenching, wrapping, sharpening)
  - Repairing (the blacksmith is repairing weapons for customers, same subtasks as Forging)
  - Transacting (allows the player to buy/sell items)
  - Wandering (the blacksmith is wandering around the town, perhaps taking a break from work or looking for inspiration)
  - Sleeping (the blacksmith is sleeping in their home, not available for interactions)
  - GoingToWork (the blacksmith is walking to their workplace, not available for interactions)
  - GoingHome (the blacksmith is walking home from work, not available for interactions)
Other details:
  - The blacksmith will have a schedule that determines when they are in each mode. For example, they might be in the Idle mode from 8am to 9am, then switch to Forging mode from 9am to 5pm, 
  then switch to Transacting mode from 5pm to 6pm, then switch to Wandering mode from 6pm to 10pm, and finally switch to Sleeping mode from 10pm to 8am.
  - The blacksmith's behaviors will be triggered by the player's presence and actions. For example, if the player enters the blacksmith's shop while they are in Forging mode, the blacksmith
  will continue forging but will also greet the player and offer to sell them weapons after they finish the current subtask. This implies that the blacksmith's behavior is self-modifying, 
  the trigger condition that checks for the player's presence will add a higher priority action that allows them to interact with the player after completing their current task.  The
  new action will be removed once the player leaves the shop or after a certain amount of time has passed without the player interacting with the blacksmith.  The new action priority will
  be higher than the current forging task but lower than the current subtask, so the blacksmith will finish their current subtask before offering to sell them weapons.
  - The blacksmith will have quests available based on the players level and progress in the game. For example, if the player has reached level 5 and has completed a certain quest, the 
  blacksmith might offer them a quest to forge a special weapon for a powerful enemy. This implies that the blacksmith's behavior is also influenced by the player's progress in the game, 
  and that certain behaviors and interactions will only be available to the player once they have reached certain milestones.
  - The blacksmith will also relay quests from other NPCs. For example, if the player has completed a quest for a farmer NPC, the blacksmith might offer them a quest to forge a tool for 
  the farmer in exchange for a reward.

Other NPC examples:
  - Farmer: has behaviors related to farming, such as planting, watering, harvesting, and selling crops. They might also have a schedule that determines when they are in each mode, such as 
  working on the farm during the day and resting at night. They might also have quests related to farming, such as asking the player to help them with a pest problem or to deliver crops to a 
  market.
  - Guard: has behaviors related to patrolling, standing guard, and responding to threats. They might have a schedule that determines when they are on duty and when they are off duty. 
  They might also have quests related to security, such as asking the player to help them investigate a crime or to protect a valuable item.
  - Merchant: has behaviors related to buying and selling items, as well as interacting with customers. They might have a schedule that determines when they are open for business and 
  when they are closed. They might also have quests related to trade, such as asking the player to deliver goods to another merchant or to find a rare item for them.
  - Armor Blacksmith: very similar to Weapons Blacksmith, except has behaviors related to forging and repairing armor
  - Tavern Keeper: has behaviors related to serving food and drinks, as well as interacting with customers. They might have a schedule that determines when they are open for business and 
  when they are closed. They might also have quests related to the tavern, such as asking the player to help them find a missing ingredient for a special dish or to deliver a message to 
  a customer.
  - Other NPCs: there are many other types of NPCs that could have a wide variety of behaviors, such as quest givers, trainers, healers, entertainers, gossipers, etc. 
  Each type of NPC would have its own unique set of behaviors and interactions with the player.

When to implement Perception and Interaction?
With behaviors implemented, we will need a way to allow the PC and Actors to interact with each other.  This will likely involve a dialogue system, which will be implemented in the next phase, 
but we can start laying the groundwork for it in this phase by allowing Actors to expose their available interactions and quests to the player when they are within a certain proximity.  
This proximity will be defined by the Perception property of the Actor, which will determine how far away they can detect the player and other WorldObjects.  When the player is within the Actor's 
Perception range, the Actor will expose their available interactions and quests to the player, allowing them to choose which ones they want to engage with.  The choices should display near the 
Actor on the map, and the player should be able to select them using the mouse or keyboard (for keyboard, the interaction list near the Actor should display a number from 1-9, initially we 
will limit the number of available interations to 9).  The available interactions and quests will be determined by the Actor's current mode and the player's progress in the game, as well as any 
other relevant conditions (such as the player's level, reputation, inventory, etc.) and the interaction list will dynamically update in real time.

OK, on to phase 13 - Serialization
Let's discuss the approach to serialization.  

I think we should have a single abstraction layer for loading and saving game data, which can support multiple formats (YAML for static data, JSON for save/load).  
This abstraction layer would be responsible for converting between the in-memory data structures (WorldData, WorldObject, Actor, Behavior, etc.) and the serialized formats.  
This would allow us to keep the loading/saving logic separate from the core game logic, and also make it easier to switch out or add new serialization formats in the future if needed.

The player will have a username, and each game they create will have a player character name that is stored with the that save state.  
The player will also have a current level, progress in the game, gold, inventory (.etc) which will also be stored in the save state.  
I recommend that we have a single directory to store all game data, including the starting world data (NPC definitions, dialogue trees, item definitions, etc.) and the players save states.

There are 3 types of data we need to serialize:
1) Static data: this includes things like the world layout, NPC definitions, dialogue trees, item definitions, etc.  This data is read-only and will be loaded at the start of the game.  
We need a format like YAML for this, which is human-readable and easy to edit.
I suggest we use this filename template for static data: StaticData[data name].yaml.  
For example, StaticData[NPCDefinitions].yaml, StaticData[DialogueTrees].yaml, StaticData[ItemDefinitions].yaml, etc.
2) Dynamic save state data: this includes things like the current state of the world, the player's inventory, the NPCs' current modes and tasks, etc.  
This data is read-write and needs to be loaded once then saved during the game.  When the player starts a new game, we need to copy the game starting (default) data to the players 
save state.  Then we can load the players save state normally.  Assume we have one directory to store data, players save states should be prefixed with the , and any other files we need to persist.
We need a format like JSON for this, which is faster for machine parsing than YAML.
I suggest we use this filename template for dynamic data: [username]_[charactername]_[save timestamp].json.
For example, if the player is named "Alice" and their character is named "Warrior", their save state file might be named "Alice_Warrior_2024-06-01T12-00-00.json".
3) Starting world data: this includes the default state of the world when the player starts a new game.  This data is read-only and will be copied to the players save state when they start a new game.
We need a format like JSON for this, which is faster for machine parsing than YAML.
I suggest we use this filename template for starting world data: StartGameData[data name].yaml.
For example, if we have a default starting state that we want to copy to the players save state when they start a new game, we might name it StartGameData[InitialState].yaml.

Please summarize the key concerns and tradeoffs, and propose a strategy for how to implement it in a way that is maintainable, flexible, and efficient.  
Consider things like: performance, ease of use, compatibility with existing data structures, and support for multiple formats.
